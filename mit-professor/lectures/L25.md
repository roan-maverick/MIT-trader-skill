# Lecture 25: Ross recovery theorem

Instructors: Peter Carr and Jiming Yu (Morgan Stanley). Talk title: "Can we recover?"

## The ideas
- Three measures: P (real-world probabilities), Q (risk-neutral, under which deflated prices are martingales; it exists if there is no arbitrage) and the prices of Arrow-Debreu securities (claims paying 1 in one state).
- Option prices reveal Q (Lecture 20), not P. The difference between them is the market's risk premium.
- Steve Ross's recovery theorem: if the economy is a Markov chain with finitely many states and the pricing kernel has a particular (transition-independent) form, then P can be recovered uniquely from a matrix of state prices, using the Perron-Frobenius theorem (Lecture 2): the real-world probabilities come from its positive eigenvector.
- The speakers examine the assumptions critically and show alternative conditions; recovery is possible but rests on strong assumptions.
- Related: the growth-optimal (Kelly) portfolio, whose mean long-run growth rate is the largest of all portfolios, connects to these measure changes.

## Watch out
- "The options market says a 22% chance" is a price, not a forecast. Recovering the forecast needs assumptions no one can check.
