# Lecture 20: Option price and probability duality

Instructor: Stephen Blythe (Harvard Management Company). MIT OCW has the video and transcript but no notes.

## The ideas
- Option prices and probability distributions are two descriptions of the same object. No option pricing formula is needed to see it.
- Start with three assets: a call, a zero-coupon bond paying 1 at T, and a digital option paying 1 if the price ends above K.
- A tight call spread (long the K call, short the K + dK call, scaled by 1/dK) pays like a digital. So the digital's price is minus the derivative of the call price in the strike.
- Dividing by the bond price turns that into a probability: the risk-neutral probability of finishing above K.
- A butterfly (the second difference of calls in the strike) isolates a narrow range of outcomes: its price is the risk-neutral density.

## Formulas
- P(S_T > K) = -(1 / Z) dC/dK, density f(K) = (1 / Z) d2C/dK2, with Z = e^{-rT} the bond price (Breeden-Litzenberger).
