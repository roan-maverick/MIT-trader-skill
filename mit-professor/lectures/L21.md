# Lecture 21: Stochastic differential equations

Instructor: Choongbum Lee.

## The ideas
- An SDE dX = mu(t, X) dt + sigma(t, X) dB describes a process by its local drift and noise.
- Solving some SDEs exactly: coefficient matching with Ito's lemma (GBM is solved this way).
- The Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process dX = kappa (theta - X) dt + sigma dB: pulled back to theta at speed kappa. Its exact discrete version is an AR(1) with phi = e^{-kappa dt}.
- Numerical methods when no solution exists: finite differences (Euler), Monte Carlo simulation (average many simulated paths), and trees (a binomial approximation of Brownian motion).
- The heat equation and its link to Black-Scholes (a change of variables turns one into the other).

## Formulas
- OU half-life ln 2 / kappa. Stationary standard deviation sigma / sqrt(2 kappa).
- Exact OU step: X_{t+dt} = theta + (X_t - theta) e^{-kappa dt} + sigma sqrt((1 - e^{-2 kappa dt}) / (2 kappa)) Z.

- Monte Carlo error shrinks like 1 / sqrt(paths): 10,000 paths give probabilities to about +/- 1 percentage point.
