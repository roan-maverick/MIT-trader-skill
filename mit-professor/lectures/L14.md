# Lecture 14: Portfolio theory

Instructor: Peter Kempthorne. Case Study 6 (US sector ETFs, optimal portfolios with 30% and 15% caps).

## The ideas
- Markowitz mean-variance analysis: choose weights w to get the highest expected return for a given variance, or the lowest variance for a given return. The answers form the efficient frontier.
- Two assets show the whole idea: with correlation below 1, a mix has less risk than the weighted average of the risks. With strong negative correlation, risk can nearly vanish.
- Adding a risk-free asset: the best risky portfolio is the tangency portfolio (highest Sharpe ratio); everyone mixes it with cash (the capital market line).
- Constraints (no shorting, caps per asset) matter in practice and change the answer.

## Formulas
- Portfolio mean w'mu, variance w'Sw. Minimum variance (unconstrained): w proportional to S^{-1} 1. Tangency: w proportional to S^{-1}(mu - r_f).
