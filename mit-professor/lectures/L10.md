# Lecture 10: Regularized pricing and risk models

Instructor: Ivan Masyukov (Morgan Stanley).

## The ideas
- Bonds, swaps, discount factors and the yield curve. Fitting a curve exactly to every quote gives wild, unstable forward rates.
- Regularization: fit the data well AND keep the answer smooth by adding a penalty (Tikhonov) on roughness. The penalty weight trades accuracy for stability.
- The same idea builds a stable volatility surface from scattered option quotes.
- Regularized models give stable risk numbers: small changes in inputs give small changes in hedges.

## Formulas
- Minimize ||A x - b||^2 + lambda ||D x||^2 (D a difference operator). Solution x = (A'A + lambda D'D)^{-1} A'b.

## In this pack
- The same principle, applied to covariance matrices: Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage, a weighted average of the noisy sample covariance and a simple target, with the weight chosen from the data. Used by /mit-portfolio and /mit-var.
- /mit-portfolio meanvar shrinks past mean returns halfway to their average for the same reason.

## Watch out
- Unregularized optimizers maximize estimation error: they pile into the assets whose past numbers were luckiest.
