# Lecture 26: Introduction to counterparty credit risk, and course conclusions

Instructor: Yi Tang (Morgan Stanley); closing remarks by Kempthorne, Lee, Strela and Xia.

## The ideas
- Counterparty credit risk: in an over-the-counter derivative, the other side may default when it owes you money.
- Exposure is random: it depends on the future market value of the trade. Profiles: expected exposure over time, expected positive exposure (EPE), potential future exposure (a high percentile).
- Credit valuation adjustment (CVA): the price of that risk, roughly the loss given default times the integral of expected exposure against the counterparty's default probabilities (from CDS). DVA is the same from the other side.
- Collateral and netting reduce exposure. Wrong-way risk: exposure grows exactly when the counterparty is most likely to default.
- Computing it for a whole firm means simulating every trade in one consistent Monte Carlo: an enterprise-level modeling problem.

## Formulas
- CVA ~ (1 - recovery) x sum over time of EE(t) x [probability of default in (t-1, t)] x discount.

## In the skill
- Background. The lesson for a personal account: a broker is a counterparty too; keep cash and positions within protected limits, and size every risk so one failure cannot end the game.
