# Lecture 8: Time series analysis I

Instructor: Peter Kempthorne. Case Study 3 (US 10-year Treasury yields).

## The ideas
- Stationarity: the statistical properties do not change over time (strictly, or in mean and covariance).
- Wold's theorem: any covariance-stationary series is a (possibly infinite) moving average of white-noise shocks plus a predictable part.
- ARMA(p, q): AR terms (past values) and MA terms (past shocks). AR(1): X_t = c + phi X_{t-1} + e_t is stationary when |phi| < 1. Yule-Walker equations link AR coefficients to autocorrelations.
- Non-stationary series: trends and unit roots (phi = 1, a random walk). ARIMA differences them.
- Dickey-Fuller and augmented Dickey-Fuller tests: is phi = 1? Regress the change on the lagged level; the t-statistic follows a special (non-normal) distribution.

## Formulas
- Mean-reversion half-life of an AR(1): ln(0.5) / ln(phi) periods.
- ADF regression: dX_t = a + gamma X_{t-1} + sum b_k dX_{t-k} + e_t; reject a unit root when the t of gamma is below about -2.86 (5%, with a constant).

## In the skill
- scan.py reports the OU (continuous AR(1)) half-life of each log price.
- pairs.py runs the ADF on the spread (with Engle-Granger critical values).

## Watch out
- Stock prices almost never pass a unit-root test: they wander. That is why single-stock "mean reversion" is weak and pairs exist.
