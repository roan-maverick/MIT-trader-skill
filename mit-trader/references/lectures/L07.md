# Lecture 7: Value at Risk (VaR) models

Instructor: Kenneth Abbott (Morgan Stanley).

## The ideas
- Risk management's job is "no surprises": management must know the risk profile, and large concentrated losses must be prevented.
- VaR estimates a bad-but-not-the-worst loss, for example the 1-day loss exceeded on only 1% of days.
- Use returns, not prices: they are closer to stationary and comparable across assets.
- Three methods: variance-covariance (fast, assumes normality and linear positions), historical simulation (replay real past days on today's positions, keeps fat tails and real correlations), Monte Carlo (simulate from a model, handles non-linear positions).
- Sensitivities by asset type: equity exposure, interest-rate duration and PV01, credit spread duration, commodity exposure; correlations across them matter most in a crisis.
- Backtesting: count the days the loss beat the VaR and test whether the count is plausible (Kupiec).

## Formulas
- Parametric: VaR = z_conf x sqrt(v' S v) for dollar exposures v. z = 2.33 at 99%.
- Expected shortfall: the average loss on days beyond the VaR; for a normal, sd x phi(z) / (1 - conf).

## In the skill
- var.py computes all three plus expected shortfall and a Kupiec backtest; check.py blocks orders that push VaR over max_var_pct.

## Watch out
- VaR says nothing about how bad the worst 1% is. Correlations rise in a crash, so diversification shrinks exactly when it is needed.
