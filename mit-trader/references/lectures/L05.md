# Lecture 5: Stochastic processes I

Instructor: Choongbum Lee.

## The ideas
- A stochastic process is a random path. The simple random walk adds independent +1/-1 steps; its increments are independent and stationary, and X_n / sqrt(n) tends to a normal.
- Gambler's ruin: a fair random walk starting at 0 hits +A before -B with probability B / (A + B).
- Markov chains: the future depends on the present only. A transition matrix, and under mild conditions a stationary distribution (an eigenvector, Lecture 2).
- Martingales: a fair game, E[X_t | the past] = X_s. A random walk is one.
- Optional stopping theorem: if you stop a martingale at a (bounded) stopping time, its expected value is still the starting value. No stopping rule turns a fair game into a winning one.

## Why it is the most important lecture for traders
- Put a stop and a target on a price that is a martingale. Whatever the distances, the expected profit is zero. Stops and targets change the shape of the outcomes (many small losses and a few big wins, or the reverse), never the average.
- Gambler's ruin gives the shape: a target twice as far as the stop is hit first about one time in three, which is exactly what keeps the expected value at zero.
