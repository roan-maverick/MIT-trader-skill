# Lecture 1: Introduction, financial terms and concepts

Instructors: Peter Kempthorne, Choongbum Lee, Vasily Strela, Jake Xia (Jake Xia's opening talk on how financial products are traded).

## The ideas
- Markets: exchanges and over-the-counter; stocks, bonds and credit, currencies, commodities, real estate and mortgages, derivatives.
- Who trades and why: investors with money who need a return, borrowers who need money and carry risk. Dealers and market makers quote bid and ask and manage an inventory of risk; hedgers remove risk they do not want; proprietary traders and fund managers take risk on purpose.
- Three jobs for the mathematics: pricing (relative value, no arbitrage), risk management (because people are risk averse and markets punish greed and fear), and trading strategies (where the talk warns against believing in a "holy grail" or a perpetual-motion robo-trader).
- Alpha and beta come from one regression of an asset's return on a benchmark's: R(a) = alpha + beta R(b). Beta is the part you could get from the benchmark; alpha is the rest.
- The Greeks (delta, gamma, theta, vega) are a dealer's way of describing how a book of options changes.
- Behavior matters: people take a gamble to avoid a sure loss but take a sure gain over a gamble with the same expected value. Long market cycles and short memories lead to extrapolating the recent past.
