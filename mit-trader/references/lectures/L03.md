# Lecture 3: Probability theory

Instructor: Choongbum Lee.

## The ideas
- Discrete and continuous random variables, expectation, independence, the cumulative distribution function.
- Normal distribution N(mu, sigma^2). Prices are better modeled as lognormal: the log of the price, not the price, is normal, so prices stay positive and returns compound.
- Moment generating function M(t) = E[e^{tX}]: its derivatives at 0 are the moments. The lognormal has no MGF, a first hint that heavy tails behave differently.
- Law of large numbers: the average of many independent draws converges to the mean (why the casino always wins in the long run).
- Central limit theorem: the standardized sum of many independent draws is approximately normal, whatever the draws look like. It is what makes t-statistics and confidence intervals work.

## Formulas
- z = (x - mean) / standard deviation.
- If log S_T ~ N(mu, sigma^2) then E[S_T] = exp(mu + sigma^2/2).
- Standard error of a mean of n draws: sigma / sqrt(n).

## In the skill
- scan.py's dip and rip setups are z-scores of the log price.
- signal.py's t-statistics and the Monte Carlo margin of error (2 standard errors) rest on the CLT.

## Watch out
- Daily returns have fat tails (excess kurtosis 3 to 10 is common; NVDA's was about 5). A "3-sigma day" happens far more often than a normal distribution says.
