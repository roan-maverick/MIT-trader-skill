# Lecture 24: HJM model for interest rates and credit

Instructor: Denis Gorokhov (Morgan Stanley).

## The ideas
- Banks trade hundreds of derivative types and need one framework flexible enough to price new payoffs quickly. Monte Carlo is that framework, and HJM fits it.
- Heath-Jarrow-Morton models the whole forward-rate curve directly. No-arbitrage forces the drift of each forward rate to be determined by the volatilities (the HJM drift condition), so only volatilities must be chosen.
- Short-rate models (for example Hull-White) are special cases.
- The same structure applies to credit: model forward hazard rates and survival probabilities, then price CDS and credit derivatives in one simulation.

## Formulas
- Bond price P(t, T) = exp(-integral_t^T f(t, u) du). Under the risk-neutral measure the drift of f(t, T) is sigma(t, T) integral_t^T sigma(t, u) du.

## In the skill
- Background only. TLT and IEF (long and medium Treasury ETFs) in the universe are the retail way to hold rate risk.
