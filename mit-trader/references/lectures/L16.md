# Lecture 16: Portfolio management

Instructor: Jake Xia.

## The ideas
- Portfolio construction in practice, explained through special cases of Markowitz: two assets, equal correlations, the classic 60/40 stocks/bonds mix that most managers are compared against.
- Beta and the Sharpe ratio: return above cash per unit of risk, the "risk-adjusted return". Alpha is what is earned beyond the beta exposure.
- Kelly's formula: with a known edge, the bet size that maximizes long-run growth. A 50/50 even-money bet deserves nothing; certainty deserves everything.
- Risk parity: weight assets so each contributes the same risk, rather than the same dollars. Bonds get more money, equities less; often leveraged to reach a return target.
- Limitations: all of it depends on estimated volatilities and correlations, which change, and on assumptions about the future.

## Formulas
- Sharpe = (R_p - R_f) / sigma_p. Kelly for a bet paying b per 1 with win probability p: f = p - (1 - p) / b. For a general outcome R: f ~ E[R] / E[R^2].
- Risk contribution of asset i: w_i (S w)_i / (w'Sw). Risk parity sets them equal.

## Watch out
- Full Kelly with an overestimated edge is a fast way to ruin. Half Kelly keeps about three quarters of the growth with half the volatility.
