# Lecture 13: Commodity models

Instructor: Alexander Eydeland (Morgan Stanley).

## The ideas
- Commodity prices depend on storage and delivery. In contango (later futures above spot) someone with storage can buy now, store, and sell forward, locking in a profit if the spread beats storage and financing costs.
- Storage is an option: its value can be replicated with calendar spread options on futures.
- Energy and power: prices mean-revert, spike (jumps) and have strong seasonality; spark spreads (power minus fuel) value power plants as options.
- Models need mean reversion, jumps and several factors; the forward curve's shape matters as much as its level.

## In the skill
- Background. XLE, GLD and GDX in the default universe are commodity-linked ETFs; gold and miners are a classic pair (pairs.py).

## Watch out
- Commodity ETFs that hold futures lose money in steady contango from rolling contracts, even if spot is flat.
