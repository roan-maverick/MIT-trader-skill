# Lecture 9: Volatility modeling

Instructor: Peter Kempthorne. Case Study 4 (GARCH on exchange rates).

## The ideas
- Geometric Brownian motion: constant volatility; estimate it from returns at any time scale.
- Sample volatility estimators and how they scale with time.
- Jumps: a Poisson jump-diffusion adds sudden moves; fitted by maximum likelihood.
- ARCH and GARCH: today's variance depends on yesterday's squared return and yesterday's variance. This produces volatility clustering and fat-tailed unconditional returns even with normal shocks.
- Testing for ARCH effects: Lagrange multiplier test / autocorrelation of squared returns. Fitting by maximum likelihood. Goodness of fit (Kolmogorov-Smirnov), non-Gaussian shocks (t-distribution), and extensions (asymmetric responses).

## Formulas
- GARCH(1,1): sigma^2_t = omega + alpha r^2_{t-1} + beta sigma^2_{t-1}.
- Long-run variance omega / (1 - alpha - beta); a shock's half-life ln 0.5 / ln(alpha + beta).
- k-day-ahead variance: long-run + (alpha + beta)^k (next - long-run).

## In the skill
- vol.py fits GARCH(1,1) by full Gaussian MLE, forecasts the h-day vol and sets stops in its units.
- montecarlo.py's default model simulates GARCH with the stock's own standardized shocks (fat tails kept).

## Watch out
- Volatility forecasts are much better than return forecasts. That asymmetry is the core of risk management: you can size risk even when you cannot predict direction.
