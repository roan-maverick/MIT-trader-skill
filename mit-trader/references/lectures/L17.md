## The ideas
- Brownian motion as the limit of a scaled random walk: continuous paths, independent normal increments with variance equal to elapsed time (B_t - B_s ~ N(0, t - s)).
- Strange paths: it crosses zero infinitely often near the start, stays near the curve x = +/- sqrt(t), and is nowhere differentiable. Its quadratic variation over [0, t] is t, the fact that makes Ito calculus different.
- The reflection principle: the probability that Brownian motion reached level a by time t is twice the probability it ends above a. Running maximum and first-passage times follow.

## Formulas
- P(max_{s<=t} B_s >= a) = 2 P(B_t >= a).
- Brownian bridge: a path from x0 to x1 over a step of variance v touched a barrier b (beyond both ends) with probability exp(-2 (x0 - b)(x1 - b) / v).

## In the skill
- montecarlo.py uses the bridge formula, so a daily simulation still counts stops touched during the day.
- Rule of thumb: the chance of touching a level 1 sigma away within the horizon is about 2 N(-1) ~ 32%, not 16%.
