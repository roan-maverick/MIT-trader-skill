# Lecture 22: Calculus of variations and its application in FX execution

Instructor: Eric Pan (Morgan Stanley). MIT OpenCourseWare publishes no notes or video for this session; this note follows the standard formulation of the problem in the title.

## The ideas
- A large order moves the price (market impact). Trading it fast costs impact; trading it slowly exposes it to price moves while it waits.
- Choosing the best trading path x(t) over all possible paths is a calculus-of-variations problem: minimize an integral of a cost that depends on x and its speed.
- The Euler-Lagrange equation gives the optimal path. With quadratic impact cost and a penalty on the variance of the result, it is x'' = kappa^2 x, solved by hyperbolic sines (Almgren and Chriss).

## Formulas
- Minimize E[cost] + lambda Var[cost] with E[cost] ~ eta integral (x')^2 dt and Var ~ sigma^2 integral x^2 dt.
- kappa^2 = lambda sigma^2 / eta; x(t) = X sinh(kappa (T - t)) / sinh(kappa T). kappa -> 0: a straight line (TWAP).

## Watch out
- For orders far under 1% of a day's volume, impact is small and the plan is one order.
