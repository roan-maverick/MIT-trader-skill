# Lecture 19: Black-Scholes formula and risk-neutral valuation

Instructor: Vasily Strela.

## The ideas
- Replication: a forward contract can be replicated by borrowing to buy the stock; its price is forced by no arbitrage, not by anyone's forecast.
- An option can be replicated by continuously rebalancing a position in the stock and in cash (delta hedging). The cost of that replication is the option's price.
- So the price does not depend on the stock's expected return. It is the expected payoff under the risk-neutral measure (where the stock grows at the risk-free rate), discounted.
- The Black-Scholes PDE and its closed-form solution; the Greeks as hedge ratios.

## Formulas
- Call = S N(d1) - K e^{-rT} N(d2); d1 = [ln(S/K) + (r + sigma^2/2) T] / (sigma sqrt T), d2 = d1 - sigma sqrt T.
- Delta = N(d1); gamma = phi(d1) / (S sigma sqrt T); put-call parity C - P = S - K e^{-rT}.
